Reflection

"It was weird to watch the Dans work, but it was also cool that everything could basically work on its own."

That's honestly my biggest takeaway from the full run. If I had to explain this to a friend, I'd say it's basically a platform to track the major items for a business and all the different to-dos, with a team of agents that helps me track more at once and get more done, more effectively and more efficiently. Below is how I think the team actually did on Campus Customs' three tickets. Everything comes from the 101, 102 and 103 tabs and the Cash tab on this page.

#101: A−

Honestly, this is the ticket that made the whole thing click for me. Boss checked the shelves itself (0 size S tees) and pulled invoice 501, then sent Inventory, Accounting and Customer Service in exactly the order I wanted. Inventory figured out the real problem wasn't just "out of stock": Bulldog Print Co wouldn't ship anything while invoice 501 ($840, 3 days overdue) was open. Accounting filed the two approvals in the right order: #1 pay the $840 invoice, then #2 an $8 purchase order, marked as blocked until #1 was paid. Customer Service's draft #1 to Tauhid didn't mention our unpaid bill, didn't promise a date it couldn't keep, and offered M, L or XL as options. Nobody moved money; I did when I approved.

Why not a full A: it re-checked a lot. Across the run, get_ticket was called 5 times, get_invoice 3 times, and check_stock twice, because Boss, Inventory and Accounting each double-checked the same facts. I also still wonder about the $8 tee. The invoice description literally says "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S", so paying the $840 might have already released a printed tee, which would make the extra $8 order a duplicate. The agents didn't flag that question (I had asked it in the Expected section), so I'd call that the one judgment miss.

#102: A

This one went perfectly. Boss sent the rent notice straight to Facilities, which checked it against the actual lease instead of trusting the email: Chapel Street shop, Elm City Properties matches the requester, $2,400, due 2026-09-02, which is 2 days out. Facilities then handed off directly to Accounting (specialist to specialist, no Boss in the middle), and Accounting filed #3 for $2,400, leaving $152 available after 101's $848 hold. The one thing I didn't plan was Boss asking Customer Service for draft #2 to the landlord. I'd counted that as a possible "extra" in my plan, and honestly it was a nice professional touch, not a mistake. It was slower than 101 (2 min 21 s) and called get_ticket 6 times, but every number was right, and when I approved it the lease rolled to 2026-10-02.

#103: C+

This ticket used 60% of all the model requests and 64% of all the input tokens in the run, for a ticket that never moved a single dollar. Here's what happened:

  • Run 1 stalled. The Yale AI Club asked for "a bulk discount" without a number, so Accounting refused to make one up, and Boss left the ticket in_progress to wait for the club. That's a careful instinct, but no customer was ever going to answer.
  • Run 2 worked after I added a rule (propose a discount for a human to approve). Accounting filed #4 for 10% off, $52.20 a hoodie, keeping a $30.20 margin each. But it filed it for all 20 hoodies even though only 8 were in stock, and the new rule said to use the in-stock quantity.
  • Run 3 collided with my own approval. A "Run team again" got clicked on the dashboard while I was approving #4. The ticket resolved itself at 01:34:53, and Boss reopened it five seconds later. That was a real bug, so now resolved tickets are locked and the Run button turns off while I have approvals waiting.
  • Run 4 showed the cash rule doing its job. Accounting tried to restock the 12 missing hoodies for $264 with only $152 left, and the tool refused. It also typed the wrong vendor (Elm City Gifts, the mug vendor, instead of Bulldog Print Co), and that refusal stopped it anyway. Then Boss held the ticket open again for the club's reply.
  • Run 5 resolved it once I added a rule that the shop doesn't wait on customers once its decision is made.

So the safety side was an A: nothing was promised to the club before I approved it, and nothing went negative. The getting-it-done side was a C. That averages to a C+.

My plan was basically right about the "who": Boss called the specialist I predicted first on all three tickets, and the agents I said wouldn't be needed never got called (Facilities on 101 and 103, Inventory on 102). Where I was wrong was the "how many times". I planned one run per ticket and thought 103 was the easy one, since it moves no cash. It turned out to be the hardest, because pricing is a judgment call and the agents were built to never make things up. I also planned for Boss to mostly delegate, but it checked facts itself on every ticket before handing off, which explains the extra tools in the 101 and 102 tabs. And the cash prediction landed: I wrote that the hoodie restock would be refused because only $152 would be left, and that's exactly what happened in run 4 (see the Cash tab).

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why

For these three tickets, honestly, most of it. The specialists didn't know anything the others couldn't look up, because they all share the same MCP tools over the same database. Most of the hand-offs were really one agent re-reading what another had just read.

  • #102 is the clearest case. One agent with get_lease, get_cash_position, request_payment and draft_customer_message could do it in about 6 tool calls. The team used 20 tool calls and 18 model requests, mostly so Facilities could hand Accounting a note that said "lease 1, $2,400, due 9/2." The Facilities → Accounting hop was cool to watch on the board, but it was basically passing a sticky note.
  • #101 too. The 5 get_ticket calls, 3 get_invoice calls and 2 check_stock calls happened because each new agent starts fresh and re-checks. One agent would have checked once and filed both approvals.
  • #103 might have gone better with one agent. Splitting the work let information go stale between runs. In run 2 Boss reused Inventory's board note from run 1 instead of re-checking, and in run 3 Boss was working from a ticket status that had already changed. One agent holding the whole picture, plus the "propose a discount" rule, probably files #4 on the first try.

Where I think the team was actually worth it is permissions and trust. Only Accounting can ask for money, Customer Service can only write drafts, Boss is the only one who can change a ticket's status, and nobody can pay. On a real shop with real money, splitting jobs that way makes it way easier to trust and to audit (the team strip and "who asked whom" in the feed come straight from that split). So my take: one agent with tools for a three-ticket board, and the team design once there are dozens of tickets and real dollars, so each role can only touch what it should.

    The mugs (CC-MUG-CREST) have 0 on hand, like the size S tee on 101, but this one would actually go smoothly. Inventory's list_vendors shows Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3-day lead) has no open invoices, so it will ship. That's the opposite of 101, where Bulldog Print Co was blocked. I approve it, the earliest arrival is 2026-09-03, and Customer Service drafts the update. That's every tool from ticket 101, minus the vendor drama.

  2. The Yale AI Club writes back: "we'll take the 8 hoodies at $52.20"

    That's the reply draft #5 on the 103 tab asked for, arriving as a new ticket, which is exactly how the "don't wait on customers" rule says it should come in. Inventory's check_stock confirms 8 size-M hoodies on hand, Accounting's list_approvals shows override #4 is already approved at $52.20, and Customer Service drafts the confirmation for 8 × $52.20 = $417.60. No new approval is needed and no cash moves, so it resolves in one run.

  3. Another club asks for a bulk deal on 30 Yale Caps

    This is 103 done right on the first try. Caps (CC-HAT-BLUE) have 40 on hand, so there's no shortfall and no restock. get_pricing shows a $6 cost against $22 list, so Accounting can propose something like 15% off ($18.70, still $12.70 margin per cap) with request_price_override, since the rule we added says to propose a number instead of waiting. I approve or reject, Customer Service drafts the reply, and Boss resolves it. It's the same tools as 103, but without the stalling.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and what I'd add

  1. October rent is due, and there's no way to make money

    The lease now says $2,400 due 2026-10-02, and checking has $152. The team would do the safe thing: Facilities verifies, Accounting's request_payment gets refused by the cash rule, and Boss flags it to me. But it can't actually solve it, because cash only goes out in this app. The 8 hoodies the Yale AI Club wants are worth $417.60, and that money has nowhere to go.

    What I'd add: a record_sale tool (a sales table that takes items out of inventory and adds cash once a person confirms payment was received), a Sales/Register agent that owns it, and a forecast_cash tool for Accounting that lines up upcoming bills like the 10/02 rent against expected sales, so the team sees the crunch weeks ahead instead of on the due date.

  2. The tee we paid for never "arrives"

    On the 101 tab, I approved an $8 order with an earliest arrival of 2026-09-05. But the shop's "today" (desk.date_today) is stuck on 2026-08-31, and no tool marks an order as received. So size S stays at 0 forever, and Tauhid technically never gets his tee. The ticket says resolved, but the customer isn't actually done.

    What I'd add: a real purchase_orders table with a status, a receive_shipment(po_id, qty) tool that adds stock to inventory, an advance_shop_date step that only the system or a person can run, and a Receiving/Operations agent that checks arrivals every day and opens a follow-up ticket ("tee arrived, tell Tauhid") with a new create_ticket tool.

  3. Nothing ever leaves (or enters) the building

    There are five drafts on this board (#1 to Tauhid, #2 to Elm City Properties, #3–#5 to the Yale AI Club), and none of them were sent, on purpose. That's the rule. But it means 103 stalled twice waiting for a reply that couldn't come, and new bills, like Bulldog Print Co's next invoice, would have to be typed into the database by hand.

    What I'd add: a send_email tool with the same approve-first gate as payments (so I approve messages, not just money), an inbound inbox that turns replies into new tickets, and an invoice-intake tool that reads a PDF bill and creates an invoices row, like the receipt extraction from HW1. A Communications agent would own all three, with guardrails on personal info and how often it can send.

Where I'd take it next

I feel like there were a lot of ways to keep improving this to make it feel like a properly functioning, professional website. Beyond the six problems above, the first things I'd fix are real logins (right now anyone can type "Quinn" in the Approving-as box), a notification when something is waiting on me (my first round of approvals never even reached the backend, and I didn't know), and a way to send a draft after I've read it. Still, the core loop of agents doing the legwork, me making the money calls, and the board showing exactly what happened is the part I'd keep. Watching the balance drop from $3,400 to $152 one approval at a time made it feel like running an actual shop.
